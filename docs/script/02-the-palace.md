# Act 2 — The Palace

The corridor is far too long to belong to a house. Portraits I do not recognize, mildewed tapestry, the same dust on everything. Halfway down, an open arch, and I come out onto a balcony.

And that is where I understand the size of the thing. Courtyards below. Walls. Rooftops. A huge gate, shut. And across the courtyard, a tower with a clock on its face, the hands stopped. All of it overgrown, all of it quiet, not a soul moving.

I was inside a castle this whole time. And it is abandoned.

I go back to the corridor. And that is when I see, at the far end, a man standing. Faded clothes, a tray held out to nobody, a thick web linking his arm to the handrail.

I call out. He does not answer. I get close, wave my hand in front of his eyes. Nothing. He is stopped mid-gesture, offering something to a person who is not there.

I hesitate. And I take what is on the tray: a wooden clipboard with a sheet clamped to it.

He wakes mid-sentence, like a man who never stopped.

> — Your day's roll, my lord.

Natural, courteous, the way he does it every day.

I step back. I ask whether he knows me.

He finds it funny, almost laughs, not getting the joke. And he says my name. The same name I just gave the book.

And then he sees that I am not joking. He looks at my face. He looks at my clothes. And then he looks around, for the first time.

He sees the dust. He sees the mildewed tapestry. He sees the web stuck to his own arm, and yanks the arm back in disgust, beating at his sleeve.

He does not know what happened. He does not know how much time has passed. He knows who he is, he knows who I am, he knows what he does every day, and nothing beyond that.

And he grabs at what is left so he does not go under:

> — The roll, my lord. Let us go by the roll. It is where we always begin.

I look at the sheet. It is blank.

And that is what breaks him for good. More than the dust, more than the web.

> — It cannot be empty. Your day was full, my lord. It was always full.

He takes the clipboard from my hand, looks closely, turns the sheet over, hands it back. He paces.

And then he sees the book under my arm. And his face lights up.

> — But my lord has the chronicle. Forgive me, I should have seen it sooner.

I tell him this book writes by itself.

> — It writes, my lord.

No, you did not understand. The letters appear on the page. With nobody there. I saw it happen.

And he, with all the patience in the world, like a man calming a child:

> — Why, it is your chronicle, my lord. That is what it is for. Were you to write it all in your own hand, when would you live the day?

I ask whether that is magic.

He almost laughs.

> — Magic? It is a book, my lord. It has been here since before me.

And he explains, the way you explain that water runs downhill: everything that happens in this kingdom goes in there. What my lord does, what changes, what is kept, what is lost. It asks nothing, it begs no leave and it passes no judgment. It only records. And it records even what my lord corrects later, because the truth of a day includes my lord having changed his mind partway through it.

And he adds:

> — Do not trouble yourself. It never fails. It has not failed once.

And then he leafs backward himself, anxious, right in front of me.

Blank. He turns more. Blank. He turns handfuls of pages at a time. Blank, blank, blank.

He stops. He says nothing for a while. And then, low:

> — This is not possible, my lord.

Silence. Then he straightens his back, because that is what he knows how to do.

> — The Herald of Time, my lord. In the clock tower, across the courtyard. He is the one who keeps your hours, your appointments, whom you were to see and when. Let us begin with him. It is the first place to check. Something must have stayed with him.

And while he speaks, the blank sheet of the roll gains a line, written by itself, slowly:

> speak with the Herald of Time

But it is not mine yet. It sits there, pale, waiting. The Steward notices my look and explains, thinking nothing of it: nothing enters your roll without your consent, my lord. The kingdom proposes. My lord is the one who accepts.

I accept. The line firms up on the sheet, and now it is mine.
