# Cast and objects

> First-person narration. Act 4 (The Gates) is still open.

| Character      | Office            | Object                | Function                                                   |
| -------------- | ----------------- | --------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| —              | you               | **The chronicle**     | records everything that happens in the kingdom, on its own |
| Steward        | runs the errands  | **The day's roll**    | what to do, with no hour set                               |
| Herald of Time | runs the hours    | **The book of hours** | what has an hour: start, duration, end                     |
| Gatekeeper     | runs what arrives | parcels and notes     | what comes from outside and waits                          |

**What the waking keeps and loses**

The same gap in everyone, you included. It is a gap in the near past, not in the self.

- **Kept.** Who you are. Your own name and face, and to the others yours as well — they know their lord on sight and never grope for the name. Their office and how it is done. How the kingdom works, the chronicle and the book of hours included, which is why none of it strikes them as remarkable.
- **Lost.** How you got here. What you were doing. How long it has been. Anything that happened in this place, up to the moment of waking.
- The others do not learn they lost anything until they look around. The Steward is courteous and ordinary until he sees the dust on his own sleeve; the Herald bows before he understands where he is standing.

**How the player is named**

- The chronicle asks in the bedroom, as a recap and not as a question: `to recap, my name is`. You already know the answer — the book is reading it back to you. The player writes the name there.
- The title comes with the Steward's first greeting in act 2. He says it the way he has said it every morning of his life; the player picks whether it is King or Queen.
- The title settles the form of address for the whole script. It is written **my lord** throughout, and reads _my lady_ for a Queen.

**Rules of the objects**

- The chronicle asks nothing. It only records — corrections included.
- The roll and the book of hours **propose**: the line shows up pale, and only becomes yours once you accept it.
- In the book of hours, the future is set and unset at will; the past can only be **corrected**.
- The bell rings because it is written in the book of hours. Nobody rings the bell.
